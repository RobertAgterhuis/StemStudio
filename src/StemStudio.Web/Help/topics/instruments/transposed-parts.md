Many instruments read music that is **written** in another key than it **sounds**: a trumpet or clarinet in B-flat, an
alto saxophone in E-flat, a horn in F. In the sheet music dialog, choose your instrument under *Part for your
instrument* and the part is written for it: the notes and the key signature are transposed (concert C major becomes A
major for an alto saxophone), and the part is named after the instrument. *Concert pitch* keeps the sounding notes.

- **New instruments**: the detail stems *strings*, *brass* and *woodwinds* suggest their own instruments. A section
  playing together is shown as its loudest line.
- **Clef** (from Advanced): choose another clef, for example the alto clef for a viola or the tenor clef for a trombone.
  Keys and piano always use two staves.
- **Range**: notes your instrument cannot play are kept, and the library says how many are out of range.
- **MIDI** files stay at concert pitch, so they sound right in every program; the score view follows the playback the
  same way for a part and for the concert score.

Limits: the transposition is exact, but the notes come from an automatic transcription; articulation, bowing and breath
marks are not notated.
